Chapter OneEighteen

Damien Steel woke before the sun on the morning of his eighteenth birthday with a grin already pulling at his mouth. For several seconds he stayed beneath the heavy blankets, staring at the dark beams crossing the ceiling while excitement moved hot and restless beneath his ribs. Snow had gathered against the lower edge of the windows during the night, and beyond the glass the Bloodmoon territory remained wrapped in the deep blue darkness that came just before winter dawn. By midnight, Damien would finally have the one thing he had waited his entire life to meet: his wolf.

Every werewolf knew what their eighteenth birthday meant, but Damien had never considered himself ordinary enough for the day to feel routine. He was the only son of Alpha Marcus Steel, heir to Bloodmoon, and from the moment he was old enough to understand words like pack, territory, and command, people had spoken about the future waiting for him. He had been trained beside warriors, educated beside council members, and taught to read territorial maps before most boys his age had stopped getting into trouble for sneaking out after curfew. Someday Bloodmoon would be his, and Damien had never once questioned whether he deserved it.

He pushed the blankets away and crossed his room barefoot, drawing back the curtains to look over the snow-covered courtyard below. Staff members were already moving between buildings, while two warriors worked near the rear grounds where the pack would gather later that night. Everyone would be there for his Awakening, from the oldest council members to children young enough to fall asleep on their parents' shoulders before midnight arrived. Damien liked the thought of hundreds of eyes watching when the future Alpha finally met the wolf meant to stand beside him.

He also knew exactly what kind of wolf he expected. Alpha wolves were always larger than the wolves beneath them, just as their natural presence carried more weight. Kage, Zane Calder's steel-gray wolf, was already enormous enough to draw attention whenever he shifted, but Damien knew his own wolf would be larger. That was not arrogance in his mind, though he possessed plenty of that too; it was simply the order of their kind.

A knock hit the bedroom door twice before it opened without waiting for permission. Damien did not turn because there was only one person in the packhouse who had been walking into his room without an invitation since they were old enough to walk at all. Zane Calder entered carrying two mugs of coffee and a plate piled with enough breakfast for both of them, then kicked the door shut behind him with the heel of his boot. He was nineteen, one year older than Damien, the son of current Beta Cassian Calder, and had spent every one of Damien's eighteen years close enough to be considered more brother than friend.

"Happy birthday, Your Highness," Zane said, setting the plate down before offering him one of the mugs. His dark hair was still damp from a shower, and the lazy grin on his face had been getting both of them into trouble since childhood. "I thought I should come pay my respects before your ego grows large enough to require its own wing of the packhouse. Kage suggested I bring a shovel in case we have to dig everyone else out from underneath it."

Damien took the coffee and gave him an unimpressed look. "Kage is only worried because tonight he loses his position as the biggest wolf in our generation." He drank slowly, watching Zane's expression change as the wolf inside him reacted to the insult. "Tell him not to take it personally. He had a good year."

Zane laughed hard enough to nearly spill his coffee. "Kage says you have not even met your wolf yet and you are already insufferable on his behalf." His gray eyes darkened slightly as Kage moved closer to the surface, something Damien had learned to recognize long ago. "He also says being the Alpha's son does not automatically make you less annoying."

"No, but it does automatically make my wolf larger than him." Damien smiled with smug satisfaction and reached for a strip of bacon. Zane had awakened Kage the previous winter, and Damien had spent the last year watching the two of them learn how to live inside the same body. Kage was a massive steel-gray wolf, broad through the chest and shoulders, powerful enough that several older warriors had openly praised him after his first few months of training.

Damien liked Kage and respected him, but he would have been lying if he claimed he had never imagined the night his own wolf stood beside him and made Kage look merely large. Zane knew exactly what he was thinking because there was almost nothing about Damien he did not know. They had taken their first steps within months of each other, learned to fight together, broken rules together, and taken punishments from their fathers side by side so many times that Cassian once joked the boys should simply have shared a bedroom to save everyone time. Their fathers had always assumed Zane would someday become Damien's Beta, but the friendship between them had formed long before either boy understood what those titles meant.

The years had also been generous to both of them in ways neither pretended not to notice. Damien had grown tall and broad-shouldered, with dark hair, hard features, amber eyes, and the kind of face that caused women to look twice even before they remembered he was the Alpha's son. Zane was no less fortunate, with sharp gray eyes, an athletic build, and an easy grin that had gotten him invited into more bedrooms than Damien could count. Neither of them had ever suffered from a shortage of willing attention, and neither felt particularly guilty about enjoying it.

They were careful about one thing, though. Damien and Zane never promised girls relationships they did not intend to give, never spoke of futures they did not mean, and never treated the women who chose them as disposable afterward. Their encounters were pleasure, release, and the natural outlet of young male wolves carrying more energy and hormones than good sense could reasonably contain. Every woman involved knew what it was before anything began, and both men considered honesty a much cleaner arrangement than pretending casual desire was something deeper.

Zane looked Damien over above the rim of his coffee. "So, are you spending tonight alone after your Awakening, or has someone already volunteered to help you celebrate?" His grin widened when Damien's expression shifted. "Actually, never mind. I already know the answer to that."

"Several someones have volunteered," Damien said, sounding entirely too pleased with himself. He reached for another piece of bacon and leaned back against the desk. "I have not decided which invitation I am accepting yet."

"Of course you haven't." Zane gave a solemn nod as though they were discussing a matter of great political consequence. "An Alpha must weigh difficult choices carefully."

Damien threw a piece of toast at him, and Zane caught it before it hit his chest. Their laughter carried into the hallway as Damien finally sat down to finish the breakfast Zane had brought him. There was comfort in the exchange because nothing about Zane had changed simply because today mattered more than most.

By the time they went downstairs, the entire packhouse had begun transforming for the evening celebration. Evergreen branches had been woven around stone columns, long tables filled the great hall, and staff members carried enough food through the lower corridors to feed the entire territory. Bloodmoon treated the Awakening of any young wolf as important, but the Alpha heir's eighteenth birthday carried a different weight. This was the night the pack would meet the wolf destined to someday stand at the head of Bloodmoon beside Damien.

Damien enjoyed the attention without pretending otherwise. Elderly pack members stopped to embrace him, warriors clasped his shoulder, and younger wolves openly speculated about the color of his wolf while he passed. Several young women offered smiles that were considerably less innocent than the congratulations accompanying them, and Damien returned them with the easy confidence of someone well accustomed to being wanted. He knew exactly what he looked like, exactly who he was, and exactly how much both facts mattered in Bloodmoon.

His father found him shortly before noon near the great hall. Alpha Marcus Steel stood beside Beta Cassian Calder, the two men speaking quietly until Damien and Zane approached. Marcus was forty-seven, still heavily built, still dangerous, and still possessed the kind of Alpha presence that could silence a crowded room without requiring him to raise his voice. Cassian carried a quieter authority beside him, watchful and disciplined, with the same gray eyes his son had inherited.

"Enjoying the attention?" Marcus asked, though the look on his face suggested he already knew the answer. Damien gave him a small smile that made Cassian's mouth twitch. "Why wouldn't I?" he replied.

"There is the humility we worked so hard to teach him," Cassian said. Zane nodded solemnly beside Damien and added, "I tried. It never took." Damien ignored both of them because he had heard some version of the joke most of his life.

"You were the Alpha's son on your eighteenth birthday," Damien said, looking at Marcus. "Do not pretend you hated it." His father's expression shifted slightly, not quite amusement and not quite warning. "I enjoyed it," Marcus replied. "I also knew enough to be nervous."

"About getting your wolf?" Damien asked, genuinely puzzled by the idea. Marcus shook his head once. "About meeting him." That distinction seemed unnecessarily dramatic to Damien, especially after watching Zane and Kage live together for the past year.

"I have watched Zane and Kage for a year. I know what happens," Damien said. Marcus glanced toward Zane briefly before returning his attention to Damien. "You know what happened to them. You do not know what will happen to you."

"My wolf is still my wolf," Damien said. "He is part of me, and we will figure each other out." Marcus studied him long enough to become irritating. "Listen to him before you decide you know everything."

Damien almost laughed. "I have been preparing for this my entire life." Marcus's expression did not change. "That is exactly why I said it."

The answer followed Damien throughout the afternoon more than he wanted to admit. He dismissed it whenever it surfaced because he could not imagine what his father thought might happen that Damien had not already studied. First shifts hurt, young wolves sometimes fought instinct, and the first few weeks often involved emotional swings while human and wolf learned one another. Damien knew all of it and believed knowledge would be enough.

By evening, the packhouse had filled completely. Music carried through the great hall, children darted between tables until their parents caught them, and the smell of roasted meat mixed with pine and woodsmoke. Damien danced once with his mother because she insisted, listened to speeches about his childhood, and endured Zane repeating several stories Damien would have preferred forgotten. Every time Damien looked toward the clock, midnight seemed to crawl closer by inches.

Shortly after eleven, the mood changed. The music quieted, conversations softened, and families began pulling on coats before moving toward the rear grounds. The Awakening clearing stretched beneath the mountain ridge behind the packhouse, surrounded by towering pines and covered in a smooth layer of untouched snow. Torches burned around the perimeter while the full moon hung overhead, bright enough to cast long shadows across the gathering.

Chapter TwoThe Clearing

Damien stepped into the ceremonial circle with his parents while Zane and Cassian remained nearby. Every member of Bloodmoon had gathered around them, forming a wide ring beneath the moon. Damien could feel their anticipation as clearly as his own, and the knowledge fed the pride already burning inside him. This was his moment, and nothing in him believed the night could become anything other than a triumph.

Marcus placed one hand on his shoulder before stepping away. "Whatever happens, listen first," he said quietly. Damien gave him a crooked smile and glanced toward the crowd. "You really think I am going to argue with my wolf within five minutes of meeting him?" Zane made a suspicious choking sound several feet away, forcing Damien to turn and glare at him, but Zane immediately arranged his face into an expression of exaggerated innocence before Marcus removed his hand from Damien's shoulder and said, "Just remember what I said."

Damien stood alone beneath the full moon while the final minutes passed. His pulse strengthened, his skin prickled, and the cold suddenly seemed sharper against his face. He watched the second hand move toward midnight and felt excitement build until he could barely contain it. When the clock finally struck twelve, something inside Damien opened its eyes.

Power detonated through the clearing without any gradual rise or warning. One instant Damien stood beneath the full moon waiting for the pain of his first transformation, and the next a force unlike anything he had ever felt tore outward from his body. It hit every wolf in Bloodmoon at once, driving warriors, council members, parents, young adults, and children to their knees before any of them could resist the command written into their instincts. Even wolves too young to have reached their own Awakening bowed beside their families, their bodies answering an instinct older than their own bloodlines before their minds had any chance to catch up.

Zane dropped to both knees beside his father, while Cassian bowed with him and lowered his head beneath the pressure. Kage surged toward the surface of Zane's consciousness and submitted immediately, and Damien felt the massive steel-gray wolf's response even from several yards away. There was no fear in it and no hesitation, only recognition of authority so complete that Kage yielded before Zane could possibly understand why. Damien stared at his best friend kneeling in the snow and felt the first flicker of confusion beneath his excitement.

He had seen Kage refuse submission during training more times than he could count. The steel-gray wolf respected hierarchy, but he was proud and never quick to lower himself. Yet there was no resistance in him now, not even the instinctive pushback Damien had expected from a Beta wolf confronted by an unfamiliar Alpha presence. Kage had simply recognized whatever had awakened inside Damien and bowed as though the question of rank had been settled long before either of them was born.

Only Marcus remained standing. Damien's father had gone rigid beneath the pressure, his jaw locked and his shoulders tense, but he remained on his feet through sheer Alpha strength. Every other wolf in the clearing bowed toward Damien as though something inside them had been seized by an instinct older than choice. Damien's breath caught. His entire pack was on its knees in front of him, and he had no idea what he had done to put them there.

Then he felt the presence inside his mind. It was vast, so large and heavy that Damien could not understand how something with that much presence could occupy the same consciousness he had possessed alone his entire life. Darkness opened somewhere behind his thoughts, dense and ancient, and two deep crimson eyes appeared within it. Damien could not see the wolf attached to them, only those eyes watching him from somewhere he could not reach.

A low chuckle moved through Damien's mind, and there was nothing wild or confused in the sound. It was dry, controlled, and deeply amused, as though the creature inside him had been awake far longer than Damien could imagine. Damien immediately frowned at the intrusion and pushed toward the presence with the confidence of someone who had spent his entire life expecting obedience. What is so funny? he demanded.

You are, the voice answered, and Damien bristled at once. You are my wolf, he shot back, expecting that simple fact to establish the order of things. Another quiet chuckle followed before the voice replied, So I am. Damien did not appreciate the amusement in the answer.

Damien waited for the pain of transformation, but nothing happened. His bones remained human, his hands remained hands, and no instinct surged forward demanding release. He became sharply aware that hundreds of wolves were kneeling around him and waiting to see the future Alpha shift. Shift, Damien commanded.

The crimson eyes did not move. No, the wolf replied, and Damien blinked because for one ridiculous second he thought he had misunderstood. What? he demanded, only to receive the same calm answer again. No. Heat climbed his neck as humiliation hit him. You are my wolf. I am your master. Now shift, Damien ordered, putting every ounce of authority he possessed behind the command. Rook's low chuckle rolled through his mind again, carrying more amusement than anger. Just because we share the same vessel does not make you my master. You cannot control yourself yet, and I am not one to be controlled.

Damien's jaw tightened as anger began rising beneath his embarrassment. This is my Awakening. The entire pack is waiting, he insisted, certain the obvious should matter. I know, Rook replied with maddening calm, and Damien pushed harder. Then shift.

The amusement disappeared from Rook's voice. No. Not yet, he answered, and Damien clenched his fists at his sides. That was not a request, Damien warned, expecting the words to carry enough command to end the argument. The crimson eyes narrowed slightly.

And this is not a debate, Damien Steel. I will not shift, Rook said, and the certainty behind the words struck harder than shouting would have. He was not challenging Damien to prove himself stronger, nor was he inviting another command. He had simply made a decision, and for perhaps the first time in Damien's life, being the Alpha's son did absolutely nothing to change someone else's answer. Damien had no idea what to do with that.

That realization landed harder than Damien wanted to admit. His whole life, rank had opened doors, and even those willing to correct him remembered who he would someday become. Rook possessed no such caution, and worse, Damien could feel that the wolf's refusal had nothing to do with rebellion. Rook was not trying to win a contest between them; he simply did not recognize Damien's title as a reason to surrender his own judgment.

His anger surged, and the aura followed it. The pressure across the clearing deepened until several wolves bent lower against the snow, while Zane's hands touched the ground and Cassian's head dropped farther beneath the weight. Marcus still remained upright, but the strain across his face became more visible. Damien barely noticed because all of his attention remained fixed inward.

You cannot refuse me, Damien snapped. I already have, Rook answered, and Damien's temper sharpened further. I am the future Alpha of Bloodmoon. The words carried all the certainty Damien had been raised to possess. Then perhaps one day you will learn to behave like one, Rook replied, and the insult struck directly at the pride Damien had carried into the clearing. He had expected his wolf to confirm everything Bloodmoon already believed about him, not question whether he deserved any of it. Before he could stop himself, Damien's rage exploded, and the aura followed. A child cried out somewhere in the crowd, and that sound finally reached him through the anger.

Damien looked around and truly saw what his power was doing for the first time. Every wolf in Bloodmoon remained bowed beneath a weight pouring off him that he had no idea how to stop. Zane was on his knees, Cassian was on his knees, and women, warriors, elders, and children all bent beneath the pressure. Only Marcus remained standing against it, his face set with the effort of resisting what everyone else had already yielded to.

Pride moved through Damien before he could stop it because they were submitting to him, every single one of them except the Alpha himself, and it burned through him hot and satisfied, the first taste of a feeling he wouldn't have a name for until Rook gave him one. The crimson eyes sharpened immediately. And you enjoy this, Rook observed.

Damien hesitated as the satisfaction began slipping under the weight of Rook's judgment. Rook's voice became colder when he spoke again. That is why you are not ready. Damien's pride recoiled immediately at the accusation.

Ready for what? Damien demanded, still unwilling to accept that the problem could be him. Me, Rook answered, and Damien nearly laughed in disbelief. I have trained my whole life. Rook did not sound impressed. Not enough, Rook replied, and the words hit Damien with a force entirely separate from the aura surrounding them. No trainer, elder, or warrior had ever dismissed everything Damien could already do so completely. The refusal stung because some part of him had expected Rook to be impressed by what Damien had already become. Instead, the wolf seemed to consider it barely a beginning.

You have known me for less than a minute, Damien argued, while his hands tightened again and the aura increased in response. I know enough, Rook answered, and before Damien could push again, Marcus's voice cut across the clearing. "Damien. Control it."

"I do not know how," Damien admitted, hating the words the second they left his mouth. Marcus held his gaze without moving, and there was no rescue in his expression. "Then learn." The blunt answer made Damien feel even more exposed.

The answer infuriated Damien almost as much as Rook had. He turned inward again and pushed toward the crimson eyes, demanding, Stop this. Make them stand. Rook gave him nothing, and Damien's anger climbed higher. I command you to stop.

The crimson eyes remained perfectly still. You command too much, Rook replied, and Damien wanted to scream. Then tell me what to do, he demanded, already hating the fact that he needed instruction from the same wolf refusing him. Rook did not rush to answer.

Ask, Rook replied, offering nothing more. The simplicity of it made the lesson feel more humiliating than any refusal had, because Damien understood exactly what Rook required from him. He wanted Damien to lower his pride before he would help him. Damien despised the idea almost as much as he despised having no alternative.

Humiliation burned through Damien as he looked toward Zane, still bowed beside Cassian. He swallowed his pride because whatever was happening had stopped being about his embarrassment the moment children began suffering beneath it. Please, Damien finally said. The word tasted like defeat even though he knew it should not.

The cold judgment in Rook's presence eased, just slightly, the way a hand loosens once it's made its point. Breathe, Rook instructed, and Damien inhaled through his nose even though every part of him hated needing the guidance. Rook ordered him to breathe again, slower this time, and Damien obeyed. The pressure eased slightly, just enough for Damien to recognize that the aura was connected to his emotional control. The realization immediately raised more questions.

Again, Rook ordered, and Damien drew another breath before letting it out slowly. The pressure began pulling inward, retreating from the clearing by degrees as Rook's voice remained cold and steady inside him. Your anger is not authority. Your pride is not strength. If every emotion you feel becomes everyone else's burden, you are not powerful. You are uncontrolled.

Damien wanted to argue with every word, but he continued breathing instead. The crushing weight slowly receded until people around the clearing began lifting their heads, though nobody rose immediately because the entire pack remained uncertain of what might happen next. Zane looked up first, his face pale, while Kage remained close behind his eyes. Damien looked away from him as embarrassment returned with vicious force.

The silence around the clearing felt different now. Before midnight, every person there had been waiting with excitement, ready to celebrate the Alpha heir's first shift, but Damien could feel the questions replacing that anticipation. No one dared speak them while Marcus remained in the circle, yet Damien could almost hear them: why had everyone been forced down, why had Damien remained human, and what kind of wolf could produce that much power without ever showing himself? The questions scraped at Damien's pride because he had no answers to give them.

Without the anger shielding him, Damien became painfully aware of what the entire pack had just witnessed. They had come expecting to see their future Alpha shift, and instead they had watched him lose control of a power he couldn't even name yet, let alone command. He turned inward again because humiliation quickly became stubbornness. Shift.

Rook chuckled, and the sound immediately sharpened Damien's irritation. I said shift, Damien repeated, but Rook did not move. And I said no. The calmness of the refusal made it worse. Why? Damien demanded, searching the darkness for any sign that Rook might finally give him something useful. The crimson eyes remained fixed on him with infuriating patience. Damien could feel no uncertainty behind them. Whatever Rook's reason was, he had already decided it was enough.

Because I said no, Rook answered, making it clear that Damien was not going to force anything more from him tonight. Damien fought the urge to lash out again. That is not an answer, he argued, and Rook's response came with infuriating calm. It is the only answer you are receiving tonight.

Damien stared into the darkness of his own mind, unable to make out anything beyond the crimson eyes. Who are you? he asked, changing tactics because every command had failed. Rook, the wolf answered. Damien waited for more and received nothing.

Rook what? What are you? he pressed, only to receive silence. He pushed harder, asking why everyone had bowed, why Kage had submitted, and why he could feel this much power if Rook refused to shift. Rook remained quiet for so long Damien wondered whether he had withdrawn. Damien's frustration grew with every unanswered question.

When the ancient voice finally returned, it carried that same maddening calm. Questions are useful, Rook said, and Damien nearly snarled. Then answer them, he demanded, certain that sharing a mind should at least entitle him to that much. Rook appeared to disagree.

No, Rook answered, and the word came almost pleasantly, which made Damien hate it even more. The refusal also made something else painfully clear: if Damien wanted answers about the creature now living inside him, Rook had no intention of simply handing them over. Damien would have to find them himself. Rook seemed perfectly content with that arrangement.

You are doing this deliberately, Damien accused, and Rook answered without hesitation. Yes. Damien demanded to know why, but Rook's laughter returned instead of an explanation. Another question, he observed, sounding entirely too pleased that Damien had finally begun asking rather than commanding. Damien closed his eyes and fought the urge to unleash the aura again. He could feel Rook watching him do it, plainly entertained, and being enjoyed like a spectacle made restraining himself even more irritating. When Damien finally opened his eyes, most of the pack had risen, though the uneasiness in the clearing had not faded. No one seemed quite certain what they had just witnessed, and Damien had no intention of giving them more.

Marcus approached slowly and stopped several feet away. He did not comment on the failed shift or the power that had just forced the entire Bloodmoon Pack to its knees, and his expression gave Damien almost nothing to read. The only question he asked was the one Damien could answer without exposing anything more personal than necessary. "What is his name?"

"Rook," Damien answered, watching his father carefully. Marcus's composure cracked for a fraction of a second, a tightening around his eyes, gone almost before it registered. Recognition, concern, or perhaps something deeper crossed his father's face before being buried again. Damien caught it anyway and immediately narrowed his eyes.

"What?" Damien asked, already suspicious of the reaction. Marcus glanced toward the surrounding pack before returning his attention to Damien. "Not here." His tone left no room for Damien to press the matter while hundreds of pack members were still close enough to listen.

Damien understood the warning and forced himself not to continue. Whatever questions came next would not be asked in front of Bloodmoon, especially after the pack had already seen far more of their future Alpha's private struggle than Damien wanted anyone to witness. He looked past his father toward Zane, who now stood beside Cassian with Kage still close behind his eyes. There was no mockery on Zane's face anymore, only the unmistakable seriousness of someone who had just knelt to his best friend and hadn't worked out yet how to feel about it.

Damien disliked that almost as much as he disliked standing beneath the full moon in human skin. The pack had come to watch him shift, and Rook had refused. Damien had expected midnight to prove everything he already believed about himself, but instead it had given him a wolf who would not obey him, power he could barely control, and questions nobody was going to answer in front of the pack. Nothing about the night had unfolded the way Damien had expected.

He could already imagine the conversations that would begin the moment families returned to their homes. Bloodmoon would talk about the force that had driven them down, about Marcus being the only wolf who remained standing, and about the Alpha heir who had never transformed. Damien hated every part of that almost as much as he hated knowing he could not correct the story because he did not understand it himself. For the first time that day, he wanted the crowd gone so he could get Zane alone and find out what Kage had felt.

Inside his mind, Rook settled deeper into the darkness and let out another quiet chuckle. Damien could still feel the ancient weight of him there, watching and waiting without any intention of making the night easier. For the first time in Damien Steel's life, he had encountered something his name, bloodline, confidence, and commands could not move, and he had the increasingly unpleasant certainty that Rook intended to make sure he learned exactly what that meant. Whatever came next, Damien knew the easy certainty of that morning was gone.
